Tekong-Ubin Reclamation

In 1992, Chek Jawa Wetlands was slated for reclamation. Plans were revealed to the public in the year 2000. Public outcry was immense. Crowds of researchers, members of public, and volunteers from various nature groups gathered to document everything on the shore before it would get buried.

This led to a large appeal for Chek Jawa to be saved, and for the reclamation to be deferred. In December of 2001, the Ministry of National Development announced that the reclamation of Chek Jawa would be deferred. This news came in the form of a press release from MND after the then Minister for National Development, Mr Mah Bow Tan, met with representatives of nature groups in a closed-door meeting.

After the announcement of deferment, the National Parks Board (NParks) would have to create a visitor management plan to ensure that the biodiversity of Chek Jawa would not degrade due to increased foot traffic and trampling. In July 2007, a boardwalk would be constructed in the following years to further reduce foot traffic, and the public can only walk on the intertidal zone during official NParks guided walks.

Chek Jawa today still houses six habitats. The habitats include:

  • Coastal Hill Forest
  • Mangroves
  • Rocky Shore
  • Sandy Shore and Sand Bar
  • Seagrass Lagoon
  • Coral Rubble Area

Past conservation efforts include those in 1987-1991 by The Reef Survey and Conservation Project, a project undertaken by the Republic of Singapore Yacht Club (RSYC), Singapore Institute of Biology (SIBiol) and Singapore Underwater Federation (SUF). Among the results was identifying and targeting four reef areas as good enough to merit some form of conservation: St John's group of islands, Pulau Hantu and patch reefs, Pulau Semakau and patch reefs, live firing islands including Raffles Lighthouse. More details on this and other conservation efforts on the Coral Reefs of Singapore website.
