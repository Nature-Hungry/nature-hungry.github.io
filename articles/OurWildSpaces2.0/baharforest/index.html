Bahar Forest

Ecological Significance

The forests flanking Jalan Bahar sit along one of the only ecological corridors between Western and Central Catchment habitats, which continue to be compromised by the loss of sites like Tengah Forest. Although only about 0.6 ha of the land intended for upcoming development are of native-dominated composition, the characteristic streams and waterlogged swampy conditions of this landscape have given rise to unique biodiversity throughout.

In terms of dragonfly diversity at Sites A and B, recorded rarities like the Pixie (Brachygonia oculata) and the photosensitive darners (Aeshnidae) are not widely found outside Nature Reserves due to specialised microhabitat requirements. Notably, a darner species not recorded from anywhere else in Singapore was encountered within the Nanyang Technological University campus, likely drawn to the light spillover from campus buildings but originating from Bahar or its neighbouring forests. Such findings of rarities indicate there is yet more diversity to uncover in dynamic, recovering habitats, even after being subject to comprehensive EIA studies.

Flashing displays of the nationally endangered asynchronous firefly (Pteroptyx valida) were encountered across Site B, indicating suitable swamp-like habitat for the larvae of this species which usually opts for river bends and mangroves instead. Findings of thriving Harlequin butterflies, a similarly endangered species, can likely also be explained by a combination of swampy conditions and their abundant host plants in both sites.

Mammals living in the Western Catchment forests are already affected by habitat fragmentation, particularly through road collisions and barriers created by urban development. Both the elusive Leopard Cat and Sunda Pangolin are found in the Bahar forests, with pangolin roadkill recorded regularly in nearby areas, including around the NTU campus. Without protected links between Singapore’s western and central forests, these populations risk becoming increasingly isolated from other populations on the mainland.

Why is This Area Threatened?

620 ha of land intended for the upcoming Jurong Innovation District includes the CleanTech Park (Site A) and Bahar precincts (Site B) which are hence slated for clearing. Between the two sites, 52 hectares will reportedly be developed for “advanced manufacturing activities including robotics, medical technology, and smart logistics” (MTI, 3 Feb 2026). More broadly, landuse planning in the Jurong region supports its industrial intensification, given the proximity of NTU and the construction of new MRT stations intended to service this envisioned manufacturing hub.

Our Hopes for the Site

The public feedback window for the EIA on Bahar’s forests closed in December 2025. The decision to retain a proportion of land should not just prioritise native tree cover alone but reflect the importance of streams and scrubland across Site B’s extent which support specialised and rare fauna. The challenge of mitigating impacts to this biodiversity stronghold presents an opportunity to involve relevant research and nature interest groups in assessing and conserving species groups usually neglected by traditional EIAs, such as beetles and other invertebrates. Generous buffers around firefly trees should be instated not just to mitigate the effects of urban light pollution but also to protect larval habitat. Tree-planting exercises initiated to mitigate edge effects and the flushing of wildlife onto roads are also avenues for collaboration with student and nature groups in the Bahar vicinity.
