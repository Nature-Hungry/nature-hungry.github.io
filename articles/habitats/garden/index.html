Gardens and Parks

Gardens and Parks have varying levels of ecological complexity. The ends of the spectrum have the Singapore Botanic Gardens on the end of high complexity, and Jurong Central Park on the end of lower complexity. However, the least ecologically complex park still has more biodiversity than any cycling road on our Park Connector Network (PCN), as well as our Nature Ways. This is due to size. Parks are always wider or bigger, allowing for more space and at times, a conducive climate within the park.

Community Gardens

Community gardens are common, and almost every HDB estate has it. These gardens are made up of lots of ornamental flowering plants, and plants with edible fruits.

Neighbourhood Parks

Neighbourhood parks are very urban environments, with the latest addition being the expansion of the Istana Park. These parks can hold a substantial amount of migratory bird species, as the ecological structure and functional niches accommodated for are similar to the woodlands back in East Asia.

Animals Commonly Found Here

The Ornate Sunbird (male) is always seen drinking nectar from flowers.
Yellow-vented Bulbuls feed on pest bugs that feed on sap or nectar found in ornamental flowering plants.
Spotted Doves are always seen sitting on a vantage point like a lamp post, or foraging in the grass for critters to eat.
Zebra Doves have a diet very similar to the Spotted Doves, just smaller and more nimble.
Asian Glossy Starlings mostly eat fruits, but sometimes bugs when they need protein.
Javan Mynas always forage in the grass.

These birds are truly everywhere. They love to feed on anything found in these areas, especially within community gardens.

The Brown Shrike is common in areas with large expanse of grass, but also requires some form of trees or shrubs to hunt from.
Tiger Shrikes live in areas with more trees, like Jurong Lake Gardens' Forest Ramble.
Yellow-rumped Flycatchers (male here) are immensely common during their peak weeks.
The Oriental Honey Buzzard is a large raptor that eats honeycombs. These raptors are immune to bee stings!
The Asian Openbill is a bird that recently decided to make Singapore its permanent home. Commonly seen in wetlands.
The Arctic Warbler can be found anywhere, in roadside trees, community gardens, and parks.
The Gunther's Frog (Sylvirana guentheri) is a common, naturalised species. You can hear its barks at night in ponds.
The Banded Bullfrog creates a long "mwooorrrmm" sound in ponds and drainage channels, with the calls echoing out. Very distinct.
Bronzeback snakes are harmless and very small. They are commonly seen foraging near where frogs are found, but they eat bugs as well.
