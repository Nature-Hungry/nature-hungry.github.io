Photo index of cnidarians on Singapore shores

sea anemones and anemone-like animals with polyp diameter < 3cm

Size given is the diameter with tentacles expanded


Banded bead anemone

Anthopleura

1-2cm. One row of tapered tentacles with a banded pattern. Large oral relative to tentacles. Usually brownish. On rocky shores, in sand or in cracks in large boulders and sea walls. Commonly seen on our shores.

Spotted mangrove anemone

Less than 0.5cm, at low tide, a tiny blob with 'eye spots': black centre with white and orange edges. Among barnacles, wood on the high shore among mangroves.

Striped bead anemone

DD

Paracondylactis singaporensis

2-3cm. Abut 40 tentacles with dark stripes and white bars. On hard surfaces and rocky shores. Common on our Northern shores.

Branched-tentacle mangrove anemone

CR

Pelocoetes exul

2-3cm. Few transparent tentacles, that branch at least once ending in tapering tips, with narrow white rings at the branching point. Oral disk tiny. Body column pale. Sometimes seen on mudflats near mangroves and freshwater flows on our Nothern shores.

Pink-spotted bead anemone

EN

Anthopleura buddemeieri

1-2cm. Pale column with pink spots in rows. One row of tapered tentacles pale, greyish with pinkish cast. Near high water mark on bouldery rocky shore under rich coastal forest. Seen on our Southern shores.

Lined bead anemone

LC

Diadumene lineata

Less than 0.5cm, at low tide, a tiny blob with fine stripes in white, orange, yellow or red. Among barnacles, on jetty legs and other hard surfaces. Commonly seen on our Northern shores.

Big hermit-hitching anemone

Calliactis

2-3cm. Many short tapering tentacles. Oral disk with fine white stripes. Body column thick, long with broad dark bars. On shells occupied by hermit crabs. Sometimes seen on our Northern shores.

Small hermit-hitching anemone

identification*

1-2cm. Many short tapering tentacles. Oral disk white or with white radiating stripes. Body column short, smooth and not covered in sediments, pale some with faint stripes. On shells occupied by hermit crabs. Sometimes seen on our Northern shores.

Seagrass anemone

Less than 1cm. Many translucent tentacles with tiny spots, body column short with tiny bumps. Sometimes seen stuck on seagrasses on some of our shores.

Snail-hitching anemone

LC

Paraiptasia radiata

1cm. On living snails. Many long thin tentacles. Body brown with pale stripes. On shells of living snails. Sometimes seen on some of our shores.

Mini carpet anemone

LC

Stichodactyla tapetum

These are NOT sea anemones

Sea mat zoanthid

Palythoa tuberculosa*

Colony 20-40cm, encrusts rocks and rubble like a rubbery mat. Polyps 1-2cm in diameter embedded in this common tissue, short or no body column. Coral rubble, boulders. Commonly seen on our Southern shores.

Broad zoanthid

Palythoa mutuki

Colony 5-10cm, a cluster of a few individual polyps. Polyp 1-2cm with long body column, broad oral disk. On coral rubble. Commonly seen on our shores.

Button zoanthid

Zoanthus

Colony10-15cm, encrusting small stones and rubble sometimes forming dense carpet. Polyps about 1cm with long body column, oral disk appears to be split into two. Commonly seen on our shores.

Pink button zoanthid

Zoanthus vietnamensis

Colony10-15cm, encrusting small stones and rubble. Polyps about 1cm with long body column tipped in bright pink. Sometimes seen on our shores.

Ball-tip corallimorphs

Corynactis

1.5-4cm. Tentacles many, long thin, transparent with darker ball-shaped tip. Body column smooth and brightly coloured brown, blue, orange or red. Oral disk usually dark. On rocks. Seen on our Northern shores.
