Photo index of hydroids on Singapore shores

Fact sheet on hydroids in Singapore


Candy hydroid

awaiting identification

5-10cm long with small capsules along the side branches. Pink or orange. On boulders, jetties. Commonly seen on some of our Northern shores.

Delicate hydroid

awaiting identification*

Clusters of many tiny fans, that can cover an area of about 5-10cm. Each tiny fan about 0.5cm in diameter. Pale brown. Colonies of these tiny animals are often encountered on our Southern shores, but often overlooked. In coral rubble areas.

Fluffy hydroid

awaiting identification*

10-15cm long densely branching with fine 'stems'. Usually bright orange. On coral rubble. Sometimes seen on some of our Northern shores.

Giant hydroid

awaiting identification*

2m or longer. Unbranched feather. Usually in deeper water on our Southern shores. Sometimes fragments are washed ashore.

Seagrass hydroid

awaiting identification*

0.5cm or shorter. Feathery, grows in rows on seagrass blades. Sometimes seen on some of our shores.

Fern hydroid

awaiting identification*

10-15cm long forming a fern-like shape. Usually bright orange. On boulders and coral rubble. Sometimes seen on some of our Northern shores.

Stinging hydroid

awaiting identification*

4-20cm long. Transparent or white fern-like 'leaves' on black branches. Stings powerfully. On coral rubble. Commonly seen on some of our shores.

Thread-tentacled jellyfish

Aequorea

Bell about 10cm across, thin thread-like tentacles about 25cm long. Sometimes seen on some of our shores.


These are NOT hydroids
